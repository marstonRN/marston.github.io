## Swiss health policy
No direct Swiss nursing or health-policy development was highlighted in the Federal Council or BAG release cycle this week; the main health-system narrative remained routine reporting and workforce-related discussion rather than a new policy decision ([Federal Council media releases](https://www.admin.ch/gov/en/start/documentation/media-releases.html), 4-9 November; [FOPH news](https://www.bag.admin.ch/en/current), 4-9 November).

## Nursing and healthcare in Switzerland
SBK-ASI published a cost calculator showing the estimated cost and savings from the nursing initiative package, underscoring the workforce case for stronger employment conditions and reduced turnover ([SBK-ASI](https://www.sbk-asi.ch/de/aktuell/was-bessere-arbeitsbedingungen-in-der-pflege-kosten-und-was-sie-sparen), 30 October). The Ständerat’s detailed debate on the federal law on nursing working conditions kept attention on on-call, standby and Sunday work, with SBK-ASI arguing for clearer safeguards and lower attrition pressure ([SBK-ASI](https://sbk-asi.ch/de/aktuell/sgk-s-verbesserungen-beim-pikettbereitschaftsdienst-und-der-sonntagsarbeit), 22 October). SGI-SSMI also highlighted intensive-care practice development and leadership continuity, reflecting the continued importance of interprofessional critical-care training and staffing resilience ([SGI-SSMI](https://sgi-ssmi.ch/fr/news-detail/le-gi-evolution-de-la-pratique-en-soins-intensifs-gi-epsi-se-presente), 22 October).

## Nursing and healthcare worldwide
WHO’s latest health policy work continued to emphasise primary care and access for migrants, refugees and displaced populations, underscoring the need for organised pathways and coordinated services in underserved groups ([WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240126626), 5 November). The organisation also published the mid-term evaluation of the neglected tropical diseases road map, highlighting the continued importance of sustained delivery, community engagement and supply-side resilience in global public health ([WHO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240126220), 4 November). ICN’s newsroom did not publish a distinct new statement in this period; the broader international nursing debate remained anchored in workforce conditions, education and professional protection rather than a single new event ([ICN](https://www.icn.ch/news), 4-9 November).
