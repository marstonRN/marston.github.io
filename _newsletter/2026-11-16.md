## Swiss health policy
The Swiss nursing initiative remained central to workforce policy as the SBK welcomed proposed reductions in maximum working time but said mandatory staffing standards remain the key unresolved issue ([read the statement](https://sbk-asi.ch/de/aktuell/pflegeinitiative-wichtige-fortschritte-aber-zentrale-forderungen-bleiben-unerfuellt), 11 November).
The SGI-SSMI set the agenda for the 2027 annual meeting in Lausanne, with a more focused scientific programme and stronger interprofessional exchange in intensive care practice ([read the announcement](https://sgi-ssmi.ch/fr/news-detail/nouveau-format-reunion-annuelle-de-la-ssmi-2027-22-23-09-2027-lausanne), 9 November).
Prematurity remained a recurring public-health issue in Switzerland, with Swissinfo reporting that around 5,000 babies are born prematurely each year and highlighting awareness work around World Prematurity Day ([read the article](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/demographics/around-5000-babies-are-born-prematurely-in-switzerland-every-year/92380623), 15 November).

## Nursing and healthcare in Switzerland
The SBK also marked five years of the nursing initiative with a protest walk in Bern, underlining sustained pressure for better conditions and staffing in Swiss care ([read the notice](https://sbk-asi.ch/de/aktuell/5-jahre-pflegeinitiative-sei-beim-protest-stadtrundgang-dabei), 11 November).
The intensive-care profession kept attention on quality improvement and family-facing practice through the SGI-SSMI, with a renewed emphasis on interprofessional learning and direct patient care standards ([read the profile](https://sgi-ssmi.ch/fr/news-detail/le-gi-evolution-de-la-pratique-en-soins-intensifs-gi-epsi-se-presente), 22 October).
Swiss media also kept a clear focus on child-family vulnerability, with World Prematurity Day coverage highlighting the scale of prematurity and the role of coordinated support before and after birth ([read the article](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/demographics/around-5000-babies-are-born-prematurely-in-switzerland-every-year/92380623), 15 November).

## Nursing and healthcare worldwide
WHO continued to publish major global health updates, including a 2025 survey on health and climate change and a new tuberculosis report, both of which underscore the need for stronger, more resilient health systems ([read the WHO health and climate change report](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240126466), 13 November; [read the tuberculosis report](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240126480), 12 November).
WHO also published new guidance on the management of type 1 diabetes in children and adolescents, reflecting the continued global priority of chronic disease care, screening and family support ([read the guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240126121), 13 November).

## Public debate barometer
Across Swiss media and professional discussion, debate this week centred on workforce strain and patient safety in nursing, with the SBK arguing that recent government steps on hours and funding are welcome but fail to deliver enforceable staffing standards [\[29\]](https://sbk-asi.ch/de/aktuell/pflegeinitiative-wichtige-fortschritte-aber-zentrale-forderungen-bleiben-unerfuellt), while the SGI-SSMI stressed practical ICU quality and interprofessional collaboration in the run-up to its 2027 congress [\[30\]](https://sgi-ssmi.ch/fr/news-detail/nouveau-format-reunion-annuelle-de-la-ssmi-2027-22-23-09-2027-lausanne). Coverage on premature birth and family care also remained prominent, linking early-life vulnerability to the need for better child and family support systems [\[31\]](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/demographics/around-5000-babies-are-born-prematurely-in-switzerland-every-year/92380623). *Indicative snapshot from openly accessible sources; not representative of all social media.*

## Coming up
The next focus points are likely to remain workforce planning, family-centred neonatal and ICU care, and implementation of staffing reforms in Swiss hospitals and community services.
